# Paws & Proud — Editorial Writing & Research Standard

Approved direction: 2026-11-05. Applies to all generated Dogs and Cats articles. Draft quality criteria, not a claim that an automated verifier is implemented.

## Voice
Warm, natural, specific, engaging, grounded, adult-friendly, and never formulaic. Sound like a knowledgeable pet writer explaining something useful to another pet owner. Avoid generic hooks, repetitive transitions, padded introductions, filler conclusions, robotic phrasing, unsupported certainty, and anthropomorphizing presented as scientific fact. Do not pretend AI-generated writing is firsthand experience or invent quotations, anecdotes, credentials, or sources.

## Research before writing
1. Choose a specific question that genuinely interests dog or cat owners and check for duplicates.
2. Gather relevant credible primary or authoritative sources before drafting. Prefer veterinary colleges, peer-reviewed research, recognized veterinary organizations and reliable animal-behavior resources. Date-check information where relevant.
3. Build a claim-to-source map: claim, source URL, publication date (if known), evidence strength, any limitations or disagreement.
4. Use terminology accurately. Define technical terms naturally when needed. Distinguish observed behavior, plausible explanation, clinical advice, and established evidence.
5. For medical, nutrition, toxicology, training safety, or other consequential guidance, apply stricter evidence thresholds, avoid individual diagnosis, and recommend professional veterinary advice where appropriate.

## Writing standards
- Lead with a clear, interesting, specific point rather than a generic introduction.
- Every paragraph should inform, explain, illustrate, or offer actionable advice. Delete anything that does none of these.
- Use concrete examples with relevant context; do not invent a study, firsthand story, or quote.
- Use varied but natural sentence lengths, concise headings, and readable paragraphs.
- Avoid clichés such as 'furry friends', 'in today's fast-paced world', 'it's important to note', 'unlock the secrets', and unsupported superlatives.
- No keyword stuffing, templated conclusions, repeated facts, or hollow lists.
- Friendly and feel-good, but never sacrifice accuracy to be cute.

## Automated QA gates
1. Factual claim audit against real accessible sources; broken or irrelevant citations block the draft.
2. Terminology and uncertainty review; mark contested claims and overconfident wording.
3. Filler/repetition review: flag boilerplate, redundant paragraphs, vague claims and keyword stuffing; rewrite or remove.
4. Natural-voice edit: specificity, varied rhythm, readable transitions, and practical value. No promises of undetectability or human authorship.
5. Image check: exact image-source mapping, commercial license and third-party rights considerations; no fabricated attribution.
6. Final editorial preview with title, body, image, references, and a concise quality report.
7. Only passing drafts enter owner's approval queue. Failed drafts go to automated retry or an exceptions queue, never published.
8. Owner must explicitly approve each article version. Edits after approval require renewed approval.

## Content operations
Start with one researched article per day, alternating Dogs and Cats, subject to the $15/month total operating cap and available evidence. Reduce volume instead of quality if cost is tight. No automatic publishing.
